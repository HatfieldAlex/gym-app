# 03.8 — Log exercise

**Goal:** a second button that closes out the whole movement — all N sets of it
— and hands the section back to the dropdown for the next one.

Needs chunk 03.5. Nothing is written to the API in this chunk.

## Read first

- `CurrentSession.jsx` as chunk 03.5 left it
- [00-context.md](00-context.md), assumptions A6, A7 and A10

## Build

1. **Two buttons, one line.** **Log set**, tapped once per set, and **Log
   exercise**, tapped once per movement. **Log set** is the primary of the two;
   **Log exercise** is quieter — it is the rarer tap and should read that way.

2. **Log exercise writes nothing.** Every set was saved the moment it was logged
   (A7), so finishing a movement is not a transaction: the button releases the
   hold (A10) and returns the section to "Choose an exercise". Say that in a
   comment, so the next reader does not go looking for the request. Do not add
   one, and do not add a field to `PerformedExercise` to carry it.

3. **Disabled until there is something to finish** — no sets logged against the
   held exercise, or a set in flight.

4. **One way out at a time.** 03.0's **Change exercise** now shows only while
   nothing has been logged against the held exercise; once a set exists, **Log
   exercise** is the way out. Two controls doing one thing is one too many.

5. **Finishing is not final.** Holding the same exercise again later in the
   session continues the same block (A6): its sets are still listed under it and
   a new one joins them. Nothing in the data records that a movement was
   "finished", and nothing should behave as though it did.

## Done when

- With an exercise held and nothing logged, **Log exercise** is disabled and
  **Change exercise** is the only way back.
- After one set, **Change exercise** is gone and **Log exercise** is enabled.
- Tapping **Log exercise** returns the section to the dropdown on the empty
  option, and the movement with all its sets is still in **Completed
  exercises**.
- Tapping it issues no request and creates no row.
- Holding the same exercise again brings its sets back up, and logging adds to
  the same `PerformedExercise`.
- Three exercises logged one after another read as three blocks in **Completed
  exercises**, in the order they were trained.

## Do not

- POST, PATCH or DELETE anything from this button.
- Add a `completed_at` — or any state column — to `PerformedExercise`. That is a
  backend chunk of its own, and nothing but this screen would read it.
- Put a confirmation behind it; nothing is lost by tapping it.
- Prevent returning to the exercise later, or hide a finished exercise from the
  dropdown.
- Clear or reorder anything in **Completed exercises**.

## What the user sees

Recording a workout becomes a rhythm: pick a movement, log its sets, say you are
done with it, pick the next one.

- **Two buttons sit under the inputs**, and they mean different sizes of thing:
  **Log set** records the set just performed, **Log exercise** says the movement
  as a whole is finished — all of its sets, however many that turned out to be.
- **Log exercise cannot be tapped until there is something to finish**, so an
  exercise picked by mistake is backed out of with **Change exercise** instead.
  Once the first set is logged, that small control gives way to the button: one
  way out, not two.
- **Tapping it moves the section on.** The dropdown comes back, ready for the
  next movement, and everything just logged is sitting in **Completed
  exercises** below.
- **It saves nothing, because there is nothing left to save.** Each set was
  stored as it was logged; the button marks the end of the user's attention on
  that movement, not the end of a transaction. A phone that dies between the
  last set and the tap has lost nothing.
- **Coming back to a finished exercise continues it.** Choosing bench press
  again brings its sets back up and adds to the same block — three sets before
  the squats and two after are five sets of one exercise, not two entries.
