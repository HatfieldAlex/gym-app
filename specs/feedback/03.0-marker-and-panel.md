# 03.0 — The marker and the panel

**Goal:** a marker in the top row that opens a small panel over the page, and
closes again — from anywhere in the app, without navigating, without disturbing
what is underneath.

Frontend only, and no requests: the panel is empty in this chunk. Chunk 03.5
puts the form in it. It depends on neither backend chunk and can be built
alongside them.

This is the chunk the whole feature stands on. If opening and closing the panel
costs the user their place, nothing later can fix it.

## Read first

- [Nav.jsx](../../frontend-web/src/components/Nav.jsx) — the top row
- [App.jsx](../../frontend-web/src/App.jsx) — note that `<Nav />` is rendered
  outside `<Routes>`, which is why panel state survives navigation (B6)
- [styles.css](../../frontend-web/src/styles.css) — the `nav` rules at the top,
  and the per-page sections at the bottom

## Build

1. **A new component**, `frontend-web/src/components/FeedbackMarker.jsx`,
   default-exporting `FeedbackMarker`. It owns one piece of state — whether the
   panel is open — and renders the marker button plus, when open, the panel.

2. **Mount it inside `<nav>`** in `Nav.jsx`, after the `LINKS` map, so it is the
   last thing in the top row (B3). That is the only edit to `Nav.jsx` in this
   chunk: the links themselves, their labels and their order do not change, and
   **the marker is not a `NavLink` and has no route** (B4).

3. **The marker** is a `<button type="button" className="feedback-marker">`
   carrying a glyph — `💡` — and an `aria-label` of "Log an idea or a bug",
   because a glyph is not a name. It also carries `aria-expanded={open}` and
   `aria-controls` pointing at the panel's `id`, and a `title` so a desktop
   hover explains it. Tapping it toggles the panel: open when closed, closed
   when open.

4. **The panel** is a plain `<div className="feedback-panel" role="dialog"
   aria-label="Log a note">` rendered next to the marker, containing for now a
   heading and one line of placeholder text — "Nothing to type into yet." —
   and a **Close** button. It is positioned relative to the marker so it hangs
   below the top row, over the page (chunk 06 makes that pleasant; a plainly
   positioned box is enough here).

5. **Four ways to dismiss it**, all of which must work:

   - the panel's own **Close** button,
   - the marker again,
   - **Escape** while the panel is open,
   - a click or tap **anywhere outside** the panel and the marker.

   Bind the key and outside-click listeners in an effect that only runs while
   the panel is open, and remove them on close and on unmount. An outside click
   closes the panel and otherwise behaves normally — it must not be swallowed,
   so a tap on a nav link both closes the panel and follows the link.

6. **Focus.** On open, move focus into the panel; on close, put it back on the
   marker, so a keyboard user is not dropped at the top of the document. Do
   **not** trap focus inside the panel and do not render an overlay: Tab may
   leave it, the page behind stays scrollable and clickable, and nothing is
   `inert` (B4).

7. **Nothing underneath moves.** The panel is taken out of flow — the page below
   the nav must not shift, reflow or scroll by so much as a pixel when it opens.
   Check this on a page that scrolls, mid-scroll.

8. **Style it** in `styles.css` in a new section at the bottom, `/* Feedback
   marker and panel */`, in the same voice as the sections above it: a comment
   saying *why*, then the rules. `currentColor` and `color-mix` only. It needs a
   solid background of its own, because it sits over content —
   `Canvas` is the system-aware keyword for that. Ergonomics are chunk 06;
   legible and correctly positioned is enough here.

## Done when

- The marker is visible at the end of the top row on every signed-in screen —
  Home, Current session, Exercise catalogue, Training sessions, Settings — and
  **not** on the login screen.
- Tapping it opens the panel over the page; tapping it again closes it.
- Escape closes it. Clicking outside closes it. Close closes it.
- Clicking a nav link while the panel is open both closes the panel and
  navigates.
- The URL is identical before opening, while open, and after closing, and the
  browser's Back button goes to the previous *page*, not to a closed panel.
- Scrolled halfway down Training sessions, opening the panel leaves the scroll
  position and the layout untouched.
- Navigating between pages while the panel is open leaves it open, still over
  the new page.
- Keyboard alone: Tab to the marker, Enter opens, Escape closes, and focus is
  back on the marker.
- No console warnings, and no listeners left bound after the panel closes.

## Do not

- Add a `/feedback` route, a `?feedback=1` parameter, or any other URL change
  (B4).
- Use `dialog.showModal()`, an overlay/backdrop, `inert`, or a scroll lock.
- Trap focus, or auto-open the panel on load, on a timer, or after any action.
- Put a textarea, a Send button or any request in it — chunk 03.5.
- Reorder, rename or restyle the existing nav links.
- Add an icon library or any npm dependency. The glyph is a character.

## What the user sees

**A 💡 at the end of the top row, on every screen.** It is the first visible
piece of the feature.

- **Tapping it opens a small panel** just under the top row, sitting over the
  page. The page underneath does not move, does not reload and does not lose its
  scroll position — whatever was on screen is still exactly where it was, and
  still readable around the panel.
- **The panel says there is nothing to type into yet.** That is honest: this
  chunk builds the way in and the way out, and 03.5 puts the form inside.
- **It goes away four ways** — Close, the marker again, Escape, or a tap
  anywhere else on the page — so there is no way to get stuck in it. A tap on a
  nav link dismisses it *and* takes you where you tapped.
- **The Back button still means the previous page.** Opening the panel does not
  put anything in history, so nothing has to be undone after using it.
- **It follows you.** Open it, navigate to another page, and it is still open —
  because it lives in the top row, not in the page.

Nothing can be logged yet.
